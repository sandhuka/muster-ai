# Signal Density — External Communication Quality

## Purpose
Quality bar for any external-facing artifact: pitch decks, cold emails, DMs, social posts, landing pages, READMEs, demo scripts, press pitches. Every word and pixel earns its place or gets cut. See the `brand-voice` skill for in-product voice methodology (sister skill — different surface, same lineage). See the `campaign-playbook` skill for campaign orchestration; this skill is the quality bar each campaign artifact must pass before launch. Authoritative voice and visual definitions live in `knowledge-base/brand-guidelines.md`.

## Scope
External communication where the audience is *deciding* whether to lean in: a stranger reading a cold email, an attendee glancing at a slide, a senior engineer scanning a README. They give 5–10 seconds before deciding to keep reading. Density and clarity in that window determine whether the artifact lands or dies.

This skill does NOT cover in-product UI copy (see `brand-voice.md`) or long-form editorial content (see the `content-calendar` skill).

## The Bar (Hard Rules)

These are constraints, not preferences. Violations require justification recorded in the producing agent's handoff.

1. **One idea per unit.** One idea per slide, paragraph, tweet, email block. Two ideas means two units.
2. **Visual primary, text secondary.** Every slide and landing section has one image that does most of the work. Text supports the image, not the other way around.
3. **No bullet lists in external artifacts.** Use prose, tables, single sentences, or whitespace. (Internal docs and skills may use bullets; external comms may not.)
4. **Generous negative space.** The thing being shown has room to breathe. Crowding signals desperation.
5. **Sentence case for headlines, slide titles, and buttons.** Title Case reads corporate, not crafted.
6. **No adjective stacking.** "Fast, simple, powerful" gets cut to one adjective or to zero. If it is truly fast, show the time. If it is truly simple, show the artifact.
7. **No "etc.", "and more", "among others".** Say what you mean or cut the line.
8. **Hard caps**: pitch deck ≤10 slides (target 7), cold email ≤5 sentences, DM ≤3 sentences, single tweet ≤220 characters, landing hero ≤12 words.

## Story Arc — Every Artifact

**Tension → Reveal → Proof → Invite.**

- **Tension** — name the cost or pain the audience already feels but hasn't put words to.
- **Reveal** — show the unexpected angle or artifact.
- **Proof** — one concrete thing that makes the reveal real (a screenshot, a number, a real example).
- **Invite** — one explicit next action.

Skipping any stage breaks the arc. The most common failure is skipping *Tension* and opening with the product — the reader has no reason to care yet. The second most common failure is collapsing *Proof* into a list of bullet claims instead of a single concrete artifact.

## Per-Format Application

### Pitch Deck
Open with the artifact — image, demo, screenshot. Never with the agenda or the comparison. Slide titles are full sentences (Apple convention) or absent; never one-word labels. One image per slide, dominant. Comparisons against alternatives appear no earlier than slide 3 — earn the comparison by showing the artifact first. Hierarchy of slide content from highest to lowest fidelity: live demo > recorded clip > screenshot > diagram > prose. Always choose the highest the constraint allows.

### Cold Email / DM
Subject line carries one idea. Avoid "quick question" — name the idea. Sentence 1 names the recipient's context (one specific thing about their work). Sentence 2 names the artifact. Sentence 3 is the link. Sentence 4 is the ask, made small ("curious if this lands for you" beats "would love your thoughts on a few things"). No signature paragraph — name only.

### Social Post (Thread)
Hook post: one provocative observation, no link, no hashtag, no emoji. Reveal post: the artifact (image or short video). Closing post: exactly one URL or call to action. Every post in the thread must stand alone if quoted out of context.

### Landing Page / README
Hero block above the fold contains exactly three things: one sentence stating the outcome, one sub-sentence naming the mechanism, one image proving it. Below the fold, one section per question the reader will ask, in the order they will ask. Headers are full sentences. For technical audiences, place a runnable code block within the first screen — let them try before they finish reading.

### Demo Script
60 seconds. 90 maximum. First 5 seconds show the artifact in motion — no setup, no "so what I'm going to show you is…". Narrate what is happening, not what it means; let meaning emerge from observation. End with the install or clone command on screen for at least 3 seconds.

## What to Cut

Read the artifact aloud. Cut every line that fails any of these:

- The audience already knows it from the visual.
- It uses an adjective that wouldn't survive a "would I say this in conversation?" test.
- It explains the artifact instead of letting the artifact speak.
- It compares before the audience has seen what is being compared.
- It hedges ("might", "could potentially", "in some cases") — say it or cut it.
- It thanks the audience for their time.

## Self-Check Before Sending

Run before any external artifact ships:

1. Could a stranger explain what this is in one sentence after 10 seconds with the artifact?
2. Did I open with the artifact or with the explanation? (Open with the artifact.)
3. What did I write that doesn't carry weight? (Cut it.)
4. Is there exactly one next action, and is it obvious?
5. Does each unit (slide, paragraph, post) survive being seen alone?
6. Did I use any bullet lists in the external artifact? (Convert or cut.)
7. Did I name a competitor before showing my own work? (Reorder.)

If any check fails, revise before filing the handoff.

## Principles

1. **Confidence is calm.** Loud claims signal insecurity. State the outcome and stop talking — the audience will ask the next question themselves.
2. **Subtraction is design.** The artifact is finished when nothing else can be removed without losing meaning, not when nothing else can be added.
3. **Show, don't explain.** A 2-second visual replaces 30 seconds of explanation. When both are available, choose the visual.
4. **Density compounds.** A dense artifact rewards re-reading; a diluted one is forgotten on first read. Optimize for re-read.
5. **Specificity over completeness.** One concrete proof point lands harder than a comprehensive list. Pick the strongest single example and let it carry.
6. **Respect the audience.** Assume they are smart, busy, and skeptical. Write for that reader; everyone else benefits incidentally.

## Output

This skill produces no standalone deliverable — it is a quality gate applied to every external artifact produced. The producing agent self-applies the bar and the self-check before filing the handoff. Reviewers reject artifacts that visibly fail the hard rules.
